# 2. NEW FEATURE: HUDUMA MANAGEMENT

Introduce a new service management capability inside the business administrator module.

The functionality will be integrated into the existing `Bizampia` page, specifically around the existing `Bidhaa Mpya` section.

There must be two main service management sections.

## 2.1. Huduma Mpya

Introduce a section called:

`Huduma Mpya`

When an administrator opens this section, the system should allow them to create a new service by selecting products that will be consumed when that service is performed.

### Workflow

1. Display all products registered under the current business.
2. Allow the administrator to select one or multiple products.
3. Each selected product becomes a consumable associated with the service.
4. Allow the administrator to save the service.
5. Ask for the service name and any other essential information required by the existing application design.

For example:

Service name: Hair Treatment

Consumable products:

| Product | Buying price |
|---|---:|
| Shampoo | 5,000 |
| Hair Oil | 3,000 |
| Conditioner | 4,000 |

The resulting service configuration should recognize that Hair Treatment consumes Shampoo, Hair Oil, and Conditioner.

### CRITICAL QUANTITY REQUIREMENT

The system must support defining the required quantity of each consumable per service unit.

For example:

| Product | Required quantity |
|---|---:|
| Shampoo | 2 |
| Hair Oil | 1 |
| Conditioner | 1 |

These quantities represent the default consumption recipe for one unit of the service.

Allow zero quantities where appropriate, but do not allow negative quantities.

If the business has more products, the administrator should be able to select additional products without an artificial limitation.

A product may belong to multiple services.

A product must also remain independently sellable through the existing product sales functionality.

**Important:** Do not create separate inventory records for service consumables. Service consumables must reference the existing product inventory records.

## 2.2. Huduma Zilizopo

Introduce another section called:

`Huduma Zilizopo`

When clicked, display all services registered under the current business.

For every service, display:

- Service name.
- Associated consumable products.
- Required quantity of each consumable per service unit.
- Relevant service information.

The administrator must be able to:

1. View a service.
2. Add a new product to an existing service.
3. Remove a product from an existing service.
4. Update the required quantity of an existing consumable.

The administrator must NOT be required to recreate the entire service when making these modifications.

Apply proper confirmation before deleting a service or removing a consumable.

Do not delete the actual product from inventory when removing it from a service.

### Business isolation

Services must belong to the correct business.

An administrator must never be able to view, edit, delete, or associate products belonging to another business.

Respect the existing business ownership and branch architecture.

# 3. DATABASE ARCHITECTURE

Design a proper relational database structure for services.

Do not store service consumables as comma-separated product IDs or JSON arrays when relational tables are appropriate.

A recommended structure is:

### services

- id
- business_id
- name
- description (nullable)
- status
- created_at
- updated_at

### service_consumables

- id
- service_id
- product_id
- required_quantity
- created_at
- updated_at

Adapt these structures to the existing database conventions.

Requirements:

1. Use foreign keys where appropriate.
2. Add suitable indexes.
3. Prevent duplicate product associations within the same service.
4. Ensure proper cascading or restricted deletion behavior.
5. Ensure business isolation.
6. Use decimal quantities if the existing inventory system supports fractional products.
7. Do not introduce destructive migrations.
8. Existing product records and sales history must remain untouched.

The database must also support recording historical service transactions independently of future changes to service configurations.

# 4. NEW FEATURE: UZA HUDUMA

Now integrate service selling into the seller module.

Inside the existing `Uza` page, introduce a new section called:

`Uza Huduma`

This must coexist with the existing product-selling functionality.

Do not replace or interfere with the existing product sales interface.

## 4.1. Service selection

When a seller clicks `Uza Huduma`:

1. Display all available services belonging to the seller's business.
2. Allow the seller to select the service they intend to perform.
3. Display all consumable products associated with that service.
4. Display their default required quantities.

For example:

Selected service: Hair Treatment

| Consumable | Default quantity |
|---|---:|
| Shampoo | 2 |
| Hair Oil | 1 |
| Conditioner | 1 |

## 4.2. Quantity input

The seller must be able to enter the quantity of each consumable required for the transaction.

For example, the seller may change the quantities depending on the actual service being performed.

If the selected service requires three different products, the system must provide three corresponding quantity inputs.

Allow the seller to adjust quantities according to actual usage.

The system must validate quantities against available inventory.

It must never allow stock to become negative.

If a required consumable is unavailable or insufficient, clearly inform the seller before the transaction is completed.

Do not silently ignore missing consumables.

## 4.3. Manual service pricing

The seller must manually enter the amount charged to the customer for the service.

Do not assume that a service has a fixed selling price.

The entered price will represent the total revenue generated by the service transaction.

Validate that the selling price is greater than or equal to zero, following the existing application's financial validation rules.

## 4.4. REAL-TIME PROFIT CALCULATION

This is one of the most important requirements.

The system must calculate and display the expected profit immediately as the seller enters or modifies the service price and consumable quantities.

Use the following formula:

**Total consumable cost = Sum of (actual quantity consumed × product buying price).**

**Expected service profit = Service selling price − Total consumable cost.**

Example:

| Product | Quantity | Buying price | Total cost |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Shampoo | 2 | 5,000 | 10,000 |
| Hair Oil | 1 | 3,000 | 3,000 |
| Conditioner | 1 | 4,000 | 4,000 |
| **Total** | | | **17,000** |

If the seller enters a service price of TZS 35,000:

Revenue = TZS 35,000

Consumable cost = TZS 17,000

Expected profit = TZS 18,000

Display these values dynamically while the seller is typing.

The profit preview must update whenever:

- The service price changes.
- A consumable quantity changes.
- The selected service changes.

Use frontend calculations for immediate feedback, but NEVER trust frontend calculations for financial transactions.

The backend must independently recalculate the actual cost and profit using trusted database values.

### Financial precision

Use the existing application's currency and decimal precision conventions.

Avoid floating-point arithmetic errors when calculating money.

Use appropriate decimal or integer monetary representations.

# 5. SERVICE SALE CONFIRMATION AND AUTOMATIC STOCK DEDUCTION

When the seller confirms the service sale, the backend must execute the entire transaction safely.

The process must:

1. Validate the seller's authorization.
2. Validate business and branch ownership.
3. Validate the selected service.
4. Validate the consumable products.
5. Retrieve the latest product buying prices from the database.
6. Validate the actual quantities.
7. Check available inventory.
8. Calculate total consumable cost.
9. Calculate service revenue.
10. Calculate actual profit.
11. Save the service sale.
12. Save the individual consumables used.
13. Deduct the consumed quantities from the appropriate inventory records.
14. Record the inventory movements.
15. Save the transaction date, seller, business, and branch.
16. Return a successful transaction response.

### DATABASE TRANSACTION REQUIREMENT

All financial and inventory operations must execute inside a database transaction.

Use appropriate row-level locking where supported.

If any operation fails, roll back the entire transaction.

For example, if a service requires three products and the third product has insufficient stock, the system must not deduct the first two products.

Never allow partial service transactions.

Prevent duplicate sales caused by accidental repeated submissions.

Follow the existing transaction and inventory movement conventions wherever possible.

### Stock deduction

Deduct actual quantities consumed, not simply the default service recipe quantities.

For example:

If one Hair Treatment consumes:

- Shampoo: 2 units.
- Hair Oil: 1 unit.
- Conditioner: 1 unit.

And the seller performs three Hair Treatments, the stock deductions should reflect the actual quantities recorded for all three services.

Do not deduct stock twice.

If the existing application has multiple branches, deduct stock from the correct selling branch's inventory, following its established stock management rules.

# 6. SERVICE SALES DATABASE HISTORY

Service sales must have their own properly structured transaction records, or use an existing unified sales architecture if it can safely accommodate service transactions.

Every completed service sale must preserve:

- Service ID.
- Service name snapshot.
- Business ID.
- Branch ID.
- Seller ID.
- Service quantity, if multiple service units are supported.
- Total service revenue.
- Total consumable cost.
- Calculated profit.
- Transaction date.
- Transaction status.

For each consumable used, preserve:

- Product ID.
- Product name snapshot.
- Actual consumed quantity.
- Buying price at the time of sale.
- Total cost at the time of sale.

### CRITICAL HISTORICAL DATA REQUIREMENT

Never calculate historical service profits using current product buying prices.

Suppose Shampoo originally cost TZS 5,000 and later its buying price changes to TZS 7,000.

An old service transaction must continue reporting its original cost of TZS 5,000 per unit.

Likewise, changing or deleting a service configuration must never alter historical sales records.

Historical financial reports must remain accurate permanently.
